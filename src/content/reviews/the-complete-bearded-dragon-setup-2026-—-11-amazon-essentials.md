  <div class="prose">
    <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
  </div>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>How long does the full setup take to assemble?</summary>
    <p>Plan on a full afternoon — roughly 4–6 hours from unboxing to first power-on. Then the empty 48-hour test run before your beardie enters. Don't try to do it in 30 minutes the day you pick up your dragon. The most common rookie mistake is rushing setup and discovering on day three that the basking spot reaches 120°F when the room warms up.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Is the 120-gallon overkill for a juvenile bearded dragon?</summary>
    <p>Slightly — but in a "they'll grow into it" way, not a problematic way. Juveniles in oversized enclosures can occasionally struggle to find food if it's spread too thin, so use a feeding tong or contained feeding platter rather than scattering insects around the whole tank. Otherwise, jumping straight to the 120-gal saves you from buying a 40-gallon growout tank you'll only use for 6 months.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What basking and ambient temperatures should I actually target?</summary>
    <p>For adults: basking surface 100–105°F, warm-side ambient 90–95°F, cool-side ambient 75–85°F, nighttime ambient 65–75°F (no light or heat needed if room stays above 60°F). For juveniles under a year: basking can run a few degrees hotter (105–110°F) to support faster digestion of higher protein intake. Verify with a separate infrared thermometer — the Inkbird probe reads air temp at probe location, not the basking surface itself.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>How often does the UVB bulb actually need replacement?</summary>
    <p>Every 12 months for the Arcadia ProT5 — and you should write the install date in permanent marker on the bulb itself. UVB output degrades long before the visible light dims, so a bulb that still "looks like it works" can be putting out half its rated UVB. Some keepers use a Solarmeter 6.5 (a UV index meter, ~$200) to verify output before replacement, but for most home keepers, calendar-based replacement is the simpler discipline.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Do I really need a thermostat? Can't I just unplug the bulb manually?</summary>
    <p>You really need a thermostat. Room temperatures fluctuate throughout the day — a 90°F basking spot at 9 AM can become a 115°F basking spot when afternoon sun raises ambient temps 8°F. You can't sit by the tank watching a thermometer. The Inkbird handles it automatically, and the WiFi notifications mean you know about problems instantly even when you're not home. It's $55 worth of peace of mind on a $626 setup.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Why no red night bulb? My local pet store sells them.</summary>
    <p>Older husbandry literature claimed reptiles can't see red wavelengths, so red bulbs could provide 24-hour heat without disturbing sleep. Modern vision research has shown that bearded dragons (and most reptiles) <em>can</em> see red light, and red bulbs running overnight disrupt their circadian rhythm. The current standard is a ceramic heat emitter for any nighttime supplemental heat — heat with zero visible light. Pet stores tend to stock products designed years ago.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What about live plants in the enclosure?</summary>
    <p>Optional and aesthetically beautiful, but a real maintenance commitment. Bearded-dragon-safe live plants include hibiscus, dwarf jade, prickly pear cactus (despined), and certain succulents. Avoid anything from the lily family, oleander, ivy, or any plant treated with pesticides. Most casual keepers use high-quality artificial plants — easier to clean, no soil to manage, no impaction risk if your beardie nibbles.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>What food and supplements should I buy alongside this setup?</summary>
    <p>This guide covers habitat, lights, and decor — not food. You'll separately need: a calcium supplement with vitamin D3 (Zoo Med Repti Calcium with D3 is the standard), a multivitamin (Repashy Calcium Plus or SuperVite), live insects (dubia roaches or crickets are staples — gut-loaded), and fresh greens (collards, mustard, turnip greens, dandelion). Watch for our dedicated bearded dragon food and supplement guide for the full diet build.</p>
  </details>

  <details class="faq-item">
    <summary>Can I substitute the 120-gallon with a smaller enclosure to save money?</summary>
    <p>Short-term yes, long-term no. A 40-gallon breeder ($120) can house a juvenile for 6–8 months, but you'll be doing this whole purchase again at the upgrade. Buying once costs less than buying twice. If budget is genuinely tight, the smartest cost-cutters are #11 (skip the background entirely) and #7 (skip the CHE unless you live somewhere that gets cold). Never cut #4 (UVB) or #5 (thermostat) — those are non-negotiable safety items.</p>
  </details>
